\begin{abstract}
This paper formalizes the distinction between the lambda oscillatory substrate proposed in the Wavelength-Native Signalling Protocol (WNSP) and the established concept of Zero-Point Energy (ZPE) in quantum field theory. We analyze mathematical, physical, and empirical differences and provide pathways for falsification.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The lambda substrate is defined as the fundamental oscillatory mode enabling information-bearing modulation on wavelength itself. Zero-point energy (ZPE), by contrast, arises from vacuum fluctuations in quantum fields. This paper clarifies equivalences, differences, and testable predictions.

\section{Lambda Formalism}
Define the lambda-field $\lambda(x,t)$ as an intrinsic oscillatory basis with frequency-dependent information capacity:
\begin{equation}
\lambda(x,t)=A \sin(\omega t - kx + \phi)
\end{equation}
Mass-energy coupling emerges when information modulation alters effective frequency:
\begin{equation}
\Delta E = h \, \Delta f.
\end{equation}

\section{Zero-Point Energy Background}
Zero-point energy emerges from the vacuum expectation value:
\begin{equation}
E_0 = \frac{1}{2}\hbar\omega.
\end{equation}
It does not encode information and cannot be directly modulated by classical means.

\section{Comparison}
\begin{itemize}
\item Lambda-field: deterministic oscillatory substrate; information-bearing.
\item ZPE: stochastic vacuum fluctuations; non-information-bearing.
\item Lambda-field mass effect: arises from modulation.
\item ZPE mass effect: arises from vacuum structure.
\end{itemize}

\section{Falsifiable Predictions}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Information-dependent mass-shift should scale as $\Delta m \propto \Delta f$.
\item Lambda oscillation collapse should produce measurable spectral phase shifts.
\item No equivalent behavior predicted by standard ZPE.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
Lambda-field theory is distinct from ZPE. While both involve oscillatory foundations, only lambda-field oscillation is information-active and experimentally tunable.
