The Three Equations That Changed Everything
1900: Max Planck Unlocks Energy
In Berlin, Max Planck solved the "ultraviolet catastrophe" with a radical idea: energy comes in discrete packets—quanta—proportional to frequency.

E = hf
Energy equals Planck's constant times frequency.

Nobel Prize, 1918. The universe revealed its first secret: energy is quantized.

1905: Albert Einstein Unifies Mass and Energy
In Bern, a patent clerk named Albert Einstein realized that mass and energy are the same thing, viewed differently.

E = mc²
Energy equals mass times the speed of light squared.

Nobel Prize, 1921 (for photoelectric effect, building on Planck). The universe revealed its second secret: mass IS energy.

2025: Lambda Boson Completes the Triangle
For 120 years, these equations stood side by side. Both equal E. Both describe the same universe. Yet no one asked the obvious question:

If E = hf and E = mc², what happens when we combine them?

hf = mc²
m = hf/c²
This is Lambda (Λ)—the mass-equivalent of oscillation.
